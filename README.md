## Featured Project: PiDog Robotic Platform

**Project:** SunFounder PiDog Assembly, Configuration & Testing  
**Platform:** Raspberry Pi 5 + PiDog  
**Status:** Mechanical Assembly Completed — Testing Pending

### Project Overview

Participated in the collaborative assembly of the SunFounder
PiDog, a quadruped robotic platform built around the
Raspberry Pi computing environment.

The project provides hands-on experience understanding
how mechanical components, servo motors, electronic
connections, and onboard computing systems work together
to support robotic movement and control.

### Phase 1: Mechanical Assembly

**Status: Completed**

Our team assembled the PiDog over several laboratory
sessions, following the hardware assembly process and
integrating the Raspberry Pi 5.

The assembly involved:

- Identifying and organizing mechanical components.
- Constructing the robotic body and leg assemblies.
- Installing and positioning servo motors.
- Connecting servos during assembly for calibration.
- Aligning components according to the assembly procedures.
- Integrating the Raspberry Pi 5 with the robotic platform.
- Coordinating assembly tasks with team members.
- Troubleshooting mechanical alignment and component placement.

### Assembly Experience & Observations

Due to scheduling changes and limited laboratory time,
the assembly process moved quickly.

Much of the initial work involved following detailed
mechanical instructions to complete the robot so the
class could progress to testing and programming.

However, the hands-on process provided valuable exposure
to the relationships between the robot's physical
structure, actuators, and computing components.

Servo calibration was particularly useful in demonstrating
that robotic assembly requires more than attaching
mechanical parts. Component positioning and alignment
directly influence how the robot will move once programmed.

## Reflections on Hands-On Robotics

One of the most valuable aspects of this course has been
experiencing the physical side of artificial intelligence
and robotics.

Much of my previous AI coursework has focused on software,
data analysis, machine learning, and model development.

Building the PiDog introduces an entirely different
dimension: the physical systems that must operate
correctly before software can produce meaningful actions.

The assembly process reinforced the importance of
component relationships, calibration, precision,
and collaborative troubleshooting.

It also demonstrated how the pace of a laboratory
exercise can influence the depth of understanding.

Completing an assembly is an accomplishment, but
understanding why each component is necessary and
how it contributes to the robot's operation is
the more important learning objective.

As the semester progresses, I look forward to
connecting the mechanical assembly experience
with programming, testing, and intelligent control.
